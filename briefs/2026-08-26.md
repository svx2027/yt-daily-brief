## Why it worked

**Theo — "Turn off Claude Code's Memory"**
A five-word, imperative title that inverts the expected advice — most coverage of an agent-memory feature says "turn it on," this says the opposite, which is a built-in curiosity gap. It's a 39.5-minute long-form video, consistent with Theo's usual deep-dive length, so the spike isn't a format change — it's the title/topic doing the work on a channel that already has a built-in daily audience for Claude Code coverage.

**Matthew Berman — Tibo Interview**
This is a 44-minute guest interview, a clear break from Berman's usual 13-18 minute solo news-reaction or tutorial format. The title borrows the guest's credibility ("Tibo Interview") and adds a FOMO framing ("Before Everyone Else"), which reads as an attempt to make a long-form format feel urgent rather than evergreen. Guest interviews are rare on this channel, so novelty relative to his own catalog is likely part of the draw.

**IBM Technology — "AI Model vs Agentic Harness"**
Short (8.5 minutes) and framed as a conceptual either/or question with no breaking-news hook — the same recurring template (short, question-titled, concept-explainer) that has repeatedly outperformed IBM Technology's longer news-roundup and podcast-style uploads in prior briefs. Consistency of format, not a topical spike, appears to be the driver.

## Takeaway for creators in this niche
When a topic already has daily attention on your channel (agent tooling, model releases), a plain declarative or inverted-expectation title on your standard format can outperform a bigger production swing — Theo's spike came from a title choice on his normal long-form style, not a new format. If you do break format (like Berman's guest interview), lean on the guest's name and a FOMO frame in the title to justify the length to viewers used to something shorter.
