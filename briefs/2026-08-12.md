## Why it worked

**1. Theo's "I Fixed Claude Without Touching Any Code":** Per the transcript, the video isn't about fixing a bug in Claude at all — it's Theo describing weeks of editing markdown-based configuration/skill files for his coding agent (T3 Code) and spending "6 to 10 hours" auditing agent histories to improve his own workflow. The title's literal claim ("fixed Claude... without touching any code") is a curiosity gap: it reads like a hack or exploit, but the payoff is a config-and-process story. At 51 minutes it's also longer than this channel's own 30-day average long-form runtime (~35 min), signaling a deep-dive rather than the channel's usual reactive AI-news format — the same channel's next-best recent video was a 44-minute reaction to a Meta product launch, not a personal workflow essay.

**2. Matthew Berman's "Mark Zuckerberg just called out Dario (and Anthropic)":** The video is a paragraph-by-paragraph read-through of Zuckerberg's real, just-published essay "The Future Is for Everyone," with Berman annotating where he agrees and disagrees and repeatedly contrasting Meta's open-source position against Anthropic/Dario Amodei's centralized-safety stance. The title borrows two recognizable proper nouns (Zuckerberg, Dario) and frames it as a direct callout/rivalry rather than a neutral "AI news" label — a pattern this channel has used before ("What did Anthropic do?!", "OpenAI vs Anthropic"). Publishing same-day on a freshly released primary source gave it a real news hook rather than manufactured drama.

**3. The PrimeTime's "He Saved Us All":** The title names no one — it's revealed only in the cold open that the video is a comedic "Programmer of the Month" biography of Edsger Dijkstra (shortest-path algorithm, semaphores, the "goto considered harmful" essay), a recurring bit format on this channel. Withholding the subject is a deliberate curiosity-gap choice, distinct from the channel's more descriptive titles ("Haskell is DONE," "The Great Bun Rewrite"). It published in the channel's fixed ~13:00 UTC daily slot, paired three minutes later with a 38-second Short ("AI Roasted Chats") — two simultaneous entry points into the same news cycle rather than one video carrying the whole day's upload.

## Takeaway for creators

All three of today's fastest-moving videos in this niche used a title that actively withholds or misdirects on the actual content — a "fix" that isn't a code fix, a named-individual feud framed around a real primary source, and a biography that hides its own subject — rather than a straightforwardly descriptive title. If you're weighing a clear, literal title against a curiosity-gap one for AI/dev-tools content this week, the data above suggests the gap is what's converting, provided the payoff (the actual video) still delivers real, checkable substance once someone clicks.
