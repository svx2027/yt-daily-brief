Timing: posted 19:30 UTC. Format: 13m5s, structured as history-then-technical-explainer (what durable objects actually are) rather than a quick take.

**2. IBM Technology — "What Is Loop Engineering? Building Better AI Agents"**
Packaging: coins/names a specific, searchable term ("loop engineering") and explicitly frames it as the successor to a term viewers already know ("prompt engineering"), inside this channel's standard "What Is X?" template. Hook: opens with that direct comparison, then grounds the abstract concept in one concrete example (an agent fixing a checkout service's failing unit tests) before generalizing. Per the transcript, the video is built around a compact, quotable framework — a four-stage loop (goal → action → observe → adjust) plus four named failure modes ("comprehension debt," "intent debt," "cognitive surrender," unverified code) — the kind of shareable vocabulary that tends to get referenced in other videos and comment threads. Timing: posted 11:00 UTC, this channel's standard daily slot. Format: 10m48s, within this channel's typical explainer range, with no same-day news hook at all.

**3. AICodeKing — "Opus + Sol Orchestrated Team: This BEATS every AI MODEL right now! (Tested)"**
Packaging: breaks from this channel's usual single-model review template into a head-to-head test of five lead/worker model pairings across six build tasks, extending its own "KingBench" benchmarking identity from raw models to agent orchestration setups. ## Takeaway for creators in this niche

None of today's top three rode a same-day headline — the day's biggest multiple actually came from a video published ten days after its own news peg, and the other two were a named-concept explainer and a benchmark franchise entry with no news hook at all.
