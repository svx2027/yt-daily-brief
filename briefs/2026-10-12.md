## Data source and limits

All numbers below came from live vidIQ MCP tool calls made during this run: `vidiq_channel_videos` (per-channel upload history with vidIQ's own `vph` and `breakoutScore` fields) and `vidiq_outliers` (cross-channel breakout scan), both scoped to this series' six-channel watchlist (Fireship, Theo/t3.gg, IBM Technology, AICodeKing, The PrimeTime, Matthew Berman). Snapshot taken 2026-10-12 ~04:45 UTC.

- `breakoutScore` is vidIQ's own proprietary metric, taken as-is — not independently recalculated.
- `vph` (views per hour) from vidIQ appears to be a recent trailing-window velocity read, not a lifetime average — e.g. Fireship's Oct 5 video shows vidIQ `vph` of 8,587.61, well below its lifetime average of ~13.7K views/hour since publish, so treat `vph` as "how fast it's moving right now," not "how fast it has moved overall."
- The **Outlier multiple** column is this series' own calculation: a video's vidIQ `vph` divided by the median `vph` of that channel's 10 immediately preceding tracked uploads (excluding the video itself). It is **not** a vidIQ field.
- Multiples are flagged **"fresh"** where the video is under ~48 hours old at snapshot time — `vph` reads run inflated in a video's first day or two before decaying, so a huge multiple there reflects recency more than durable overperformance. Two rows below are flagged this way.
- `breakoutScore` itself can drift between calls seconds apart as vidIQ recomputes continuously: a `vidiq_outliers` call read IBM's GPU-scaling video at 2.43, a `vidiq_channel_videos` call moments later read 2.51. The table uses the later read.

## Why it worked

**1. IBM Technology — "How AI Models Scale Beyond a Single GPU Across LLM Workloads"**
Packaging: a narrow, technical infra question rather than IBM's news-roundup format or a generic "AI explained" piece. Hook: the title itself is the thesis — no clickbait verbs, no named-drama framing — which matches this series' repeated prior finding that IBM's highest-breakout videos are usually the ones with *no* news hook at all (its weekly news roundups consistently underperform its single-topic explainers). Timing: no specific news peg, evergreen. Format: 9m04s, inside IBM's typical 8-12 minute explainer band rather than its 30+ minute roundup format.

**2. Matthew Berman — "Everything has been solved"**
Packaging: a sweeping, subject-withholding claim with no product or company named — a break from his immediately preceding two uploads ("12 Grok Bot Use Cases That Feel Illegal", "Mistral is BACK!"), both of which named the tool up front. Hook: the absolute claim ("Everything") creates a curiosity gap that a named-tool title can't. Timing: posted after a week of named-model-review content, so it reads as a deliberate format break rather than a template repeat. Format: 17m37s, shorter than the two preceding uploads (25m39s, 21m29s).

**3. The PrimeTime — "The Mathematics are Mad"**
Packaging: continues a subject-withholding/personification title template this series has tracked on this channel since August ("He Realized his Mistake," "They can't take it anymore"). Hook: personifying an abstract subject ("Mad") implies conflict without disclosing what happened. Timing: still reading 8,021 vph roughly 59 hours after publish — unusually sustained velocity at that distance for this channel. Format: 17m39s single-topic video, not one of the channel's 40+ minute "TheStandup" episodes, which this series has repeatedly found run colder.

## Takeaway for creators in this niche

Across today's top breakouts, **subject-withholding, curiosity-gap titles outperformed literal name-drop titles even when both styles broke out** — Berman's and The PrimeTime's cryptic claims posted higher breakoutScores than any of today's named-tool reviews on the same channels. If you have one genuinely surprising claim and a story people haven't already searched for, consider withholding the subject rather than naming it in the title; save literal naming for already-saturated, high-search-volume stories (a named model drop, a named company's incident) where people are actively searching that exact keyword and a vague title would cost you search traffic instead of gaining curiosity clicks.
